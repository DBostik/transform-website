If you are searching for a personal trainer who will come to your home in Chicago, you probably have a good reason. Maybe the gym has never worked for you. Maybe you have kids at home, a schedule that leaves no room for a commute, or you just do not want to work out in front of strangers.

Those are real reasons, and they deserve a straight answer instead of a sales pitch.

So here is the first thing to know: we do not do in-home sessions. We run a private personal training studio in Lakeview, at 2900 N Broadway, and we also coach people online. That means we are not neutral, and you should read this with that in mind. But it also means we have heard from a lot of people who tried in-home training before they found us, and from people who decided in-home was the better fit for them. Both answers are fine. The goal of this post is to help you pick the one you will actually stick with.

## The short answer

An in-home trainer is the right call if leaving the house is the thing that stops you. That covers new parents, people with mobility limits, and anyone whose schedule really cannot absorb travel time.

A private studio is usually the better call if your real problem is the crowd, the waiting, or the feeling of being watched at a big gym. A studio solves the privacy problem without moving training into the same room where you work, eat and relax.

And if what you really want is to train at home, without paying for someone to travel to you, online coaching with a real coach is a third option worth knowing about. More on that below.

## What each option actually is

**In-home personal training.** A trainer comes to your house, apartment or building gym. Some bring a bag of bands, a few dumbbells and a mat. Others work with whatever you already own. You save the commute, and you get complete privacy.

**Private studio.** An appointment-only training space with no open floor. You are not buying a gym membership. You book a time, show up, and the equipment and the coach are ready for you. Ours is in [Lakeview](/personal-trainer-lakeview/), at the corner of Broadway and Surf.

**Big-box gym trainer.** Worth mentioning, because many people searching for in-home training are really trying to get away from this. You keep a membership, add sessions with a trainer on the floor, and share the space with everyone else who is there at 6pm. We compared all three formats side by side in [Is Personal Training Worth It in Chicago?](/blog/is-personal-training-worth-it-in-chicago/), so this post focuses on the two private options.

## Cost: in-home usually costs more

In-home training is convenient, and convenience is priced in. Our [Chicago personal trainer cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-personal-trainer-cost-in-chicago/) puts in-home trainers at roughly $100 to $175 per hour in 2026, sometimes with a travel fee on top. That makes sense: the trainer is paying for their commute, their parking and the time between clients, and those costs end up in your rate.

At our studio, private 1-on-1 sessions are $45 to $55 each, depending on how many times a week you train. That is not a perfect comparison, because our sessions are 30 minutes, not 60. We chose that length on purpose, and we explained why in [30-minute vs 60-minute personal training](/blog/30-minute-vs-60-minute-personal-training/). Nutrition guidance and daily support between sessions are included, not sold as extras. Full current pricing is on our [studio services](/studio-services/) page.

If you get quotes from in-home trainers, ask three questions about money before you sign anything: Is there a travel fee or a minimum number of sessions? What happens if you cancel the same day? And does the price include any coaching between sessions, or only the time they are in your home?

## Equipment and space

This is where Chicago homes matter.

A lot of us live in apartments and condos with a living room that doubles as an office, a dining room and a place for the dog to sleep. In-home training can work well in that space, especially with bands, dumbbells and bodyweight work. But it has limits. There is only so much a trainer can carry up three flights of stairs, and heavier strength training, the kind that builds real muscle and protects your joints as you get older, gets harder to progress without the right equipment.

Some buildings have a fitness room, which helps. Before you commit, ask the trainer what they will bring, what they expect you to own, and how they plan to make the workouts harder as you get stronger.

At a studio, the equipment is already there, already set up, and your coach has a plan for how to use it. You do not have to move the coffee table first.

## Privacy and comfort

Both options are private. That is the main thing they have in common, and the main reason people choose either one over a regular gym.

The difference is the kind of privacy. At home, it is your space, which feels safe, but it is also full of distractions: a partner on a work call, kids who need something, a package at the door, the laundry you can see from the mat. At a private studio, the room is set aside for training. Nobody is waiting for your equipment, nobody is watching, and nothing from home is competing for your attention.

Our founder, David Bostik, weighed 346 pounds when he started his own journey, and he built the studio to be private and judgment-free because he remembers what walking into a crowded gym felt like. If that feeling is the reason you are looking at in-home training, a private studio may solve the same problem.

## Consistency: the factor that decides your results

Results come from the sessions you actually complete. Not the ones you plan, and not the ones you pay for and cancel.

In-home training has a real advantage here: when the trainer shows up at your door, it is hard to skip. For some people that is exactly the push they need.

But home has its own way of eating sessions. It is easy to cancel an appointment that happens in your own living room, because nothing feels lost. Work runs late, the kids are home sick, you are tired, and the trainer gets a text. When you have to go somewhere, the trip itself becomes a commitment, and the time is protected because it is already blocked off.

Neither one is better for everyone. Be honest about your own pattern. If you have a history of cancelling things that happen at home, a studio may serve you better. If getting out the door is the part that never happens, in-home may be worth the higher price.

## Who should choose an in-home trainer

In-home training is the better fit if:

- You have a mobility limit, injury or health situation that makes travel hard.
- You have young kids and no reliable childcare during the hours you could train.
- Your schedule leaves no room at all for travel time, even a short trip.
- You already own decent equipment, or your building has a good fitness room.
- The cost difference is not a deciding factor for you.

If that is you, go for it, and use the questions above to find a good trainer. A good in-home trainer is a better choice than a studio you never get to.

## Who should choose a private studio

A private studio is usually the better fit if:

- What you are trying to escape is the crowd and the feeling of being watched, not the trip itself.
- You want proper equipment and a coach who can progress your strength over months, not just weeks.
- You work best when training happens somewhere separate from home and work.
- You live or work close enough that the trip is short. For us, that means Lakeview, Lincoln Park and the nearby North Side neighborhoods.
- You want nutrition guidance and support between sessions as part of the plan.

Ask yourself one question: what has stopped you before?

If it was the commute, the trip or getting out of the house, look seriously at in-home training or online coaching.
